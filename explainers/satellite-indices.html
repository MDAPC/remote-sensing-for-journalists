One recipe, many indices

The most famous satellite indices all use the same simple recipe. Take two bands where a surface behaves very differently, subtract one from the other, and divide by their sum. The answer always lands between −1 and +1.

Index = A − BA + B

Why divide? Because sunlight changes

The same field looks brighter in full sun and darker in the shadow of a cloud or a hill. A simple difference changes with the light. Dividing by the sum cancels the brightness out, so the index measures the surface, not the weather. Move the slider.

    Read indices with care

    Compare, don't just measure

    A single value means little. The story is in the change: before and after a fire, this season versus last year.

    Thresholds are rough

    "Above 0.5 is healthy" is a guide, not a rule. Crops, climate and season all shift the numbers.

    Every index has blind spots

    Water confuses burn and moisture indices, and bare soil looks like buildings in NDBI. Mask what you don't want and check with true color.

    Clouds and smoke lie

    Optical indices fail under clouds and thick smoke. Use cloud-free dates, or switch to radar.
